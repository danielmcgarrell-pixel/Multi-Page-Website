Our History & Culture

Discover the history, traditions, festivals, and local flavors that make Davao a remarkable destination.

A Brief History of Davao

Long before Spanish colonization, the Davao region was home to diverse Indigenous communities, including the Bagobo, Mandaya, Mansaka, and other groups. These communities developed their own languages, traditions, agricultural practices, crafts, and systems of leadership. The region's coastal areas and fertile lands also supported trade and interaction between different communities.

During the Spanish colonial period, Spanish influence gradually expanded into parts of the region. In the late nineteenth century, settlement and agricultural development increased, and Davao became more closely connected to colonial administration and commerce. These changes affected Indigenous communities and reshaped settlement patterns throughout the region.

During the twentieth century, Davao experienced significant economic growth, population movement, and urban development. Davao City eventually became an important center for trade, agriculture, and tourism in Mindanao. Today, Davao Region reflects a mixture of Indigenous heritage, the traditions of long-established communities, and influences brought by settlers from other parts of the Philippines. Preserving this diverse heritage remains an important part of the region's cultural identity.

Cultural Highlights

Davao's cultural identity can be experienced through its festivals, traditional crafts, and distinctive local foods.

Festivals of Davao

Festivals celebrate the region's cultural diversity, agricultural abundance, and community traditions.

  • Kadayawan Festival: An annual celebration in Davao City recognizing Indigenous heritage, cultural diversity, and the abundance of agricultural produce.
  • Araw ng Davao: A celebration commemorating the founding anniversary of Davao City through civic activities and events.
  • Banana Festival: A festival associated with the municipality of Santo Tomas in Davao del Norte, celebrating the importance of banana farming to the local economy.
  • Kalivungan Festival: A cultural celebration associated with the province of Davao de Oro's neighboring province, Cotabato. It is included here as a nearby Mindanao cultural example rather than a Davao Region festival.

Traditional Crafts & Artistry

Traditional craftsmanship reflects the skills, knowledge, and artistic expression passed down through generations.

  • Inabal Weaving: Traditional abaca textile weaving associated with the Bagobo people, featuring intricate patterns and cultural significance.
  • Beadwork: Indigenous artisans create decorative beadwork used in accessories, clothing, and cultural items.
  • Basketry: Handwoven baskets made from natural materials for practical uses and traditional crafts.
  • Traditional Embroidery and Textiles: Distinctive designs and textile-making practices that reflect the identities of different Indigenous communities.

Traditional Foods & Local Specialties

Davao's food culture combines Indigenous food traditions, Filipino cooking, and ingredients grown throughout the region.

  • Durian: One of Davao's best-known fruits, valued for its distinctive aroma, creamy texture, and strong flavor.
  • Pomelo: A popular citrus fruit grown in the region and enjoyed fresh.
  • Kinilaw: A Filipino dish commonly prepared with fresh fish cured in vinegar or citrus and mixed with seasonings.
  • Grilled Tuna: A popular seafood dish reflecting Mindanao's coastal food culture.
  • Cacao and Chocolate: Davao is known for cacao cultivation and locally produced chocolate products.
